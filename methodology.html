Model testing notes

This is a transparent methodology page for the QosTil AI competition prototype.

Not a real-world accuracy claim. The 48 held-out messages and the separate hard set were written with AI assistance. They are not field-collected or independently annotated; there is zero exact word overlap with training, but the test domain and morphology are similar. Always have a fluent speaker check results.

1. What we built

We started with 802 AI-authored candidate tokens, excluded all test words, and trained on 676 labeled tokens across Kazakh (kk), Russian (ru) and English (en). We trained scikit-learn logistic regression on character-level 2–4 gram TF-IDF features. The trained model uses 4506 features; its weights are exported to model.json and run directly in your browser.

Baseline: Latin characters → English; distinctive Kazakh Cyrillic characters → Kazakh; remaining Cyrillic → Russian. This is stronger than an always-one-language baseline.

3. Mistakes (not hidden)

  • туралы — expected kk, predicted ru
  • Достар — expected kk, predicted ru
  • ма — expected kk, predicted ru
  • Жоспар — expected kk, predicted ru
  • Здесь — expected ru, predicted kk

Shared words, names, very short words, typos, uncommon slang and Kazakh transliterated into Latin are difficult. The classifier has no long-range context. “Scores” are uncalibrated classifier outputs, and orange underline means check manually.

4. What we would test next

Independently collect a small opt-in set of de-identified real school notices, ask at least two multilingual students to annotate each word, adjudicate disagreements, then evaluate once without training on those examples. Compare overall performance and Kazakh recall separately; report false alarms and specific errors. Do not collect or expose classmates' private messages without consent.
